    How to raise your Washington take-home pay

    • Contribute pre-tax. Every dollar into a 401(k) or HSA comes out of federal taxable income; the 2026 401(k) limit is $24,500.
    • Fix your W-4. Withholding is only as accurate as the form behind it — a mismatch shows up as a refund or a bill.
    • Know the FICA cap. Payroll tax on Social Security stops once year-to-date wages pass $184,500, so later paychecks jump.
    • Check the zero-tax state schedule. With no state wage tax, comparing offers comes down to federal brackets, FICA and local costs like property tax.

    Washington paycheck questions

    Does Washington have a state income tax?

    Washington does not tax wage income, so a paycheck there is reduced only by federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare.

    What is the take-home pay on $50,000 in Washington?

    A single filer in Washington keeps about $41,951 a year — $3,496 a month, or $1,614 every two weeks — after $3,820 of federal tax, $3,825 of FICA and $0 of state income tax.

    What is the take-home pay on $100,000 in Washington?

    A single filer keeps about $78,373 a year ($6,531 a month) and a married couple filing jointly keeps about $83,903. The difference is mostly federal bracket progression plus withholding on your W-4

    What else comes out of a Washington paycheck?

    Social Security at 6.2% up to the $184,500 wage base, Medicare at 1.45% with an extra 0.9% above $200,000, and any employer benefit premiums. Pre-tax retirement contributions such as a 401(k) reduce federal taxable income (2026 limit $24,500) but not FICA.
