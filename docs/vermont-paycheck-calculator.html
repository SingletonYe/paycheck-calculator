    How to raise your Vermont take-home pay

    • Contribute pre-tax. Every dollar into a 401(k) or HSA comes out of federal taxable income; the 2026 401(k) limit is $24,500.
    • Fix your W-4. Withholding is only as accurate as the form behind it — a mismatch shows up as a refund or a bill.
    • Know the FICA cap. Payroll tax on Social Security stops once year-to-date wages pass $184,500, so later paychecks jump.
    • Check the graduated state schedule. Vermont brackets mean the top rate only applies to the last slice of income.

    Vermont paycheck questions

    Does Vermont have a state income tax?

    Vermont uses a graduated income tax: 4 brackets running from 3.4% to 8.8%, with a $7,650 standard deduction for single filers ($15,300 for couples).

    What is the take-home pay on $50,000 in Vermont?

    A single filer in Vermont keeps about $40,936 a year — $3,411 a month, or $1,574 every two weeks — after $3,820 of federal tax, $3,825 of FICA and $1,419 of state income tax.

    What is the take-home pay on $100,000 in Vermont?

    A single filer keeps about $74,690 a year ($6,224 a month) and a married couple filing jointly keeps about $81,801. The difference is mostly federal bracket progression plus the state's graduated brackets

    What else comes out of a Vermont paycheck?

    Social Security at 6.2% up to the $184,500 wage base, Medicare at 1.45% with an extra 0.9% above $200,000, and any employer benefit premiums. Pre-tax retirement contributions such as a 401(k) reduce federal taxable income (2026 limit $24,500) but not FICA.
